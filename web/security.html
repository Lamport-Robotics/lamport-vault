Security

What has to be true.

A short list, on purpose. Everything the vault promises reduces to these, and everything we test is a test of one of them.

Assumptions

  • SHA-256 is a one-way function. That is the whole of the cryptography. No elliptic curve, no lattice, no pairing stands between a key and the funds.
  • The program on chain is the program in the repository. Builds are reproducible; anyone can compare the deployed bytes with a commit. On mainnet the upgrade authority is burned at deployment.
  • A one-time key is used once. The chain enforces this for execution with a per-key bitmap. Signers enforce it for signing with a durable journal written before any signature exists.
  • You keep the 24 words. A vault is quantum-safe, not owner-safe. Whoever holds the words holds the vault.

What an attacker gets, and what they don't

A quantum computer and every past signature
Hash preimages that are already public. Nothing that helps sign a new message.
A stolen, unexecuted signature
The right to submit exactly what you signed, before it expires. The message binds program, vault, key generation, action and a validity window of at most a day.
The fee-paying wallet's private key
The rent refund. The fee payer has no authority over the vault.
A reused key number on another device
Refused by the bitmap once the first is executed. Before that, only the signer's journal stands in the way, which is why signers reserve before they sign and devices sharing a key use separate lanes.
Rotating back to an old key, or closing and reopening a vault
Refused. Every past key is remembered, and a closed vault leaves a permanent marker at its address.
Your 24 words
Everything. This is the limit of any wallet, and the reason the same keys can live inside a hardware signer instead.

What has been verified

  • Three independent vector sources for the signature verifier: RFC 8554 Appendix F, Cisco's hash-sigs reference, NIST ACVP. The verifier reproduces the RFC's signatures byte for byte.
  • Fuzzing of the signature parser, the verifier, the action decoder, the key history and the leaf bitmap, on every push and weekly for longer.
  • Adversarial tests on a validator: replay, stale generations, rotating back, the last key, expired and future-dated requests, crash recovery, two devices on one key, close and reopen.
  • Deployment verification: a script that hashes the on-chain program and the local build and prints MATCH or not, with the upgrade authority and slot.
